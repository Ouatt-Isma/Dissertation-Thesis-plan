\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{chap:conclusion}
This thesis began from a single unresolved question: if a \aclu{NN} cannot verify its own predictions, and its user cannot either, on what basis should either of them trust it? The answer pursued here is that trust must be measured and produced by the system itself, not inferred after the fact, not left as a gut feeling, but propagated alongside the standard processes of data preparation, training, and inference as a first-class object. This closing chapter looks back at what was built, examines what was demonstrated, and turns to what remains open.

\section{Synthesis of Contributions}
\label{sec:conclusion-synthesis}

The four contributions introduced in \cref{chap:intro} were developed across \cref{chap:Trust_method} through \cref{chap:usecase}, each addressing a distinct facet of the overall problem. Taken together, they form a single coherent framework rather than a set of independent results, and it is worth restating how they connect.

The first contribution (C1) established the theoretical foundations on which everything else rests. The General Trust Assessment Methodology of \cref{chap:Trust_method} decomposed trust evaluation into three explicit phases: expression generation, \aclu{ATO}s quantification, and expression evaluation. The optimized Trust Network Analysis of \cref{chap:tnasl} made that methodology computationally viable on the graph structures that arise in modern Machine Learning during data collection or data processing. The introduction of the \acf{PNPS} refined the classical \acf{PPS} construct and yielded three concrete improvements: synthesis criteria that retain on the order of nine to eleven percentage points more information than Jøsang's original formulation, an analysis algorithm whose \acf{NL} updates run in constant time and deliver an empirical speed-up of roughly $2.2\times$ on identical inputs, and a new Referral-Edge Path operator that closes a long-standing gap in the discounting semantics of referral-only chains. These improvements were not pursued for their own sake. They were the prerequisite for applying \ac{SL} to graphs as large and as deeply nested as \acp{NN} systems, where the cumulative cost of repeated \ac{NL} recomputation and the cumulative loss from over-restrictive synthesis criteria would otherwise have rendered the framework impractical.

The second contribution (C2) used those foundations to construct the Parallel Trust Assessment System. PaTAS is organized around the observation that trust in a \ac{NN} output is conditioned on a chain of transitive dependencies: dataset trust conditions parameter trust, which together with input trust conditions prediction trust. The trust assessment module developed in \cref{chap:dataset-trust} addressed the first part in this chain: How to assess dataset trust? By organizing dataset properties along three axes (the property being assessed, the lifecycle stage at which evidence is collected, and the granularity at which the property lives), the framework produces structured opinions that distinguish supporting evidence, contradicting evidence, and missing evidence. The \ac{COMPAS}, \ac{GTSRB}, and CIFAR-10H applications each occupied a different cell of the design space and exercised a different quantification scheme, which made it possible to argue for the framework's generality without relying on a single example.

The trust propagation module developed in \cref{chap:patas} addressed the first and second links. The dataset opinion feeds directly into the first link of the propagation chain. During training, the Parameter-Trust Update module consumes the feature trust $T(x)$ and label trust $T(y)$ from the dataset trust as evidence for revising the opinion on each model parameter: gradient signals are conditioned on label trustworthiness via the inferential deduction operator, so that parameters updated from unreliable data accumulate disbelief or elevated uncertainty rather than high belief. 

With parameter trust established, the remaining question is how input trust flows through these parameters to produce output trust. The chapter addressed this by building on the perceptron case as a minimal working example, then generalizing 
the construction to deep architectures by associating a trust opinion of the output with every intermediate activation and by mirroring each \ac{NN} computation (the multiplication of inputs by parameters, the summation of contributions, the nonlinearity) with a corresponding \ac{SL} operator. The result was a parallel computation graph that operates alongside the \ac{NN} without modifying it. This non-intrusive design was a deliberate choice: it preserves the predictive behavior of the underlying model, it imposes no architectural constraint, and it makes the trust signal a property that can be added to an existing system rather than designed into a new one. 

The third contribution (C3), developed in \cref{chap:bb}, recognized that the propagation-based view of C2 assumes access to the model's internal structure, and that many deployment settings do not afford such access. The calibration-based formulation provided a complementary path: a single binomial opinion on the model as a whole, derived from observable input-output behavior through the alignment of predicted confidence with empirical accuracy. The use case of \cref{chap:usecase} demonstrated that the two views remain mutually consistent under controlled corruption of the data, confirming that they complement rather than duplicate each other.

The fourth contribution (C4) brought the framework to bear on a real-world classification task. The 5G network energy management use case was deliberately chosen for its tabular, modestly-sized, operationally meaningful character: it is the kind of problem where a practitioner might plausibly want to know not only what the model predicts but how much that prediction should be trusted. The empirical results validated three claims that, in their absence, would have left the rest of the thesis purely theoretical. 

First, \ac{PaTAS} opinions discriminate between correct and incorrect predictions when using accuracy as a property: the discriminative gap $\Delta b$ remained stable between $+0.228$ and $+0.237$ under feature noise and degraded faster under label noise, falling to $+0.041$ at $p = 0.30$, consistent with corruption of the supervision signal itself. Used as a selective prediction filter, \ac{PaTAS} consistently outperformed softmax-based filtering across all evaluated noise conditions. Second, calibration trust correctly diagnosed the asymmetry between the two corruption sources: belief declined modestly under feature noise but substantially under label flipping, which is the qualitative behavior one would expect from a faithful trust signal. Third, the per-prediction and model-level views moved consistently as data quality degraded, which is what is required for the two assessments to be jointly meaningful.

\section{What This Thesis Does and Does Not Address}
\label{sec:conclusion-scope}

It is worth being explicit about the scope of what was built and what was not.

The framework targets predictive \ac{NN} systems, and within that, primarily classification and regression problems where the notions of \emph{dataset}, \emph{feature}, and \emph{labeled training instance} are clearly defined. This is the setting in which dataset trustworthiness can be decomposed into properties such as balance, labeling reliability, or sampling adequacy; in which input-level trust can be assigned at the feature granularity; and in which the trust-propagation operators of \cref{chap:patas} mirror \ac{NN} computations. Generative \ac{AI} systems, including \acp{LLM} and diffusion models, fall outside this scope. They do not expose a fixed input-feature space at inference time, their training data is rarely labeled in the supervised sense, and the per-token or per-pixel granularity at which an output is generated has no obvious analogue to the per-output trust opinion that PaTAS produces. The methodological building blocks of this thesis (evidence-based opinions, fusion, discounting, and the underlying \ac{SL} calculus) remain applicable to such systems in principle, but their concrete instantiation would require new trust sources and a re-examination of what dataset trustworthiness means. This is a direction, not a contribution of this thesis.

The framework is not a replacement for accuracy. The trust signal provides a diagnostic: which predictions to rely on. The 5G use case demonstrated that PaTAS can serve as a more selective filter than softmax confidence alone, achieving approximately 94\% accuracy at 20\% coverage versus 86\% for softmax under combined noise. However, the choice of operating point on the coverage-accuracy curve remains a policy decision that depends on the deployment context and the relative cost of incorrect action versus abstention; the framework does not make this choice automatically.

\section{Limitations and Directions for Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion-future}

The most natural extensions of this work fall along seven axes, each of which builds on a specific limitation of what was developed here.

\paragraph{From classification to generative and sequential models.} The most visible scope restriction is the focus on predictive \ac{AI}. The \ac{SL} calculus does not depend on this restriction, and the opening paragraphs of \cref{chap:intro} hinted at the broader applicability of the approach. A natural next step is to investigate what trust sources are meaningful for self-supervised training corpora, how trust opinions might be defined over generated sequences rather than fixed-dimensional outputs, and whether the propagation mechanism can be lifted from feed-forward classification networks to transformer-based architectures. The structural optimization of \cref{chap:tnasl} is likely to remain useful in this regime, since attention layers produce graph structures that are even less DSPG-friendly than residual networks; the dataset-level methodology of \cref{chap:dataset-trust} will need substantial rethinking.

\paragraph{Toward explainability and accountability.} The trust signals produced by PaTAS carry information that is finer than a scalar prediction confidence. When a prediction is flagged as untrusted, the framework's intermediate state contains the contribution of each input feature, each layer, and the parameter trust associated with each computation. This information was used in this thesis to produce a single output opinion, but it can be inspected directly. A future direction is to use this intermediate state for explanation: identifying which inputs or which parameters are responsible for an untrustworthy prediction, attributing calibration failures to specific subsets of the training data, and supporting auditability requirements that emerging regulatory frameworks now make explicit.

\paragraph{Robustness, adversarial settings, and privacy.} The empirical evaluation considered stochastic noise applied to features and labels. Adversarial perturbations behave differently: they are designed to move predictions across decision boundaries with minimal perceptual change to the input, and a trust framework based on input-feature evidence may or may not detect them. The conjecture worth testing is that PaTAS, by reasoning explicitly about input-level evidence rather than about output-level confidence, is better positioned to flag adversarially perturbed inputs as untrusted, and consequently to signal that the resulting prediction should not be acted upon even when the model's softmax output remains high. Whether this conjecture survives empirically is an open question. A related direction concerns privacy: the federated quantification of \cref{chap:dataset-trust} was already designed to keep local class distributions private, and the entropy-based summarization that supported this design generalizes naturally to other dataset properties. A more systematic study of how the framework composes with differential privacy mechanisms, secure aggregation protocols, and federated learning more broadly is a direction with practical relevance.

\paragraph{Standardization and operational deployment.} A remaining piece of work concerns the trust-source catalog of \cref{chap:dataset-trust}, which was presented as extensible but for which no formal extension protocol was specified. Promoting the catalog from a curated list to a defined contract (what evidence a new trust source must produce, what quantification scheme it is compatible with, what granularity tag it carries, and how it composes with the existing fusion rules) would let practitioners extend the framework to their own settings without requiring a working knowledge of the underlying calculus. A related practical gap concerns the calibration of framework hyperparameters: the sensitivity threshold $\epsilon$, the uncertainty weight $W$, and the number of bins $M$ were set manually throughout this work. A sensitivity analysis showing how trust opinions vary with these choices, or a data-driven selection rule, would give practitioners clearer guidance for adapting the framework to new domains. 

\paragraph{Toward learned trust propagation.}
The trust propagation mechanism developed in \cref{chap:patas} derives output trust opinions analytically from input trust and parameter trust through the \ac{SL} operators of discounting and fusion. An alternative direction is to learn this mapping directly: rather than computing $T_{\hat{y}} = f(T_x, T_\theta)$ through a fixed algebraic procedure, one could train a meta-model to predict $T_{\hat{y}}$ from $(x, T_x)$ jointly, exploiting correlations between input content and input quality that the analytic operators cannot capture. This would require a labeled dataset of $(x, T_x, \text{correctness})$ triples, which the current framework can itself produce. Whether a learned propagator generalizes across architectures and noise regimes is an open empirical question, but the self-supervised nature of the training signal makes it an attractive direction.

In settings where other fusion operator is used, aleatoric uncertainty can be part of the uncertainty component of the final opinion (contradicted opinions), the framework's opinions will therefore reflect a mix of epistemic and aleatoric components that are not separately identified. Disentangling these two sources within the \ac{SL} calculus is an open theoretical problem, and connecting this work to the broader literature on uncertainty decomposition in deep learning is a natural next step.

\paragraph{Closing the loop: trust-informed training.}
In the current architecture, \ac{PaTAS} runs alongside the \ac{NN} without influencing its gradients or weight updates. The trust signal it produces is therefore purely diagnostic. A natural extension is to close this loop: using the per-sample trust opinion to reweight the training loss, down-weighting samples that \ac{PaTAS} identifies as carrying degraded or conflicting evidence. This would transform  \ac{PaTAS} from a passive observer into an active participant in training, analogous in spirit to curriculum learning~\cite{soviany2022curriculumlearningsurvey} or noise-robust loss formulations~\cite{ghosh2017robustlossfunctionslabel}, but grounded in an explicit trust calculus rather than heuristic weighting schemes.

\section{Closing Remarks}
\label{sec:conclusion-closing}
Trust in Machine Learning is, in the end, a question about evidence: what we have, what we would need, and how to act when the gap between them cannot be closed. The thesis that closes here is built on the conviction that this question can be answered with the same kind of rigor that the field already brings to accuracy. \ac{SL} provided the calculus; \acp{NN} provided the substrate; and the framework developed across the preceding chapters was an attempt to put the two together in a way that is operationally meaningful rather than merely formally elegant. Whether the attempt succeeded is a matter for the reader and for time. What it produced (a methodology for trust assessment, an optimized inference engine for the graphs that methodology requires, a system architecture that brings it into the Machine Learning pipeline, and an end-to-end demonstration that the resulting trust signals carry real diagnostic value) is offered here as a contribution to a conversation that the field is only beginning to answer with technical rigor.
